Text in mass

The punch-cutter worked in steel at the size of the finished letter, cutting each character in relief and checking it by smoke-proof against its fellows. From the punch a matrix was struck in copper, and from the matrix the type was cast. Every refinement visible in a page of 1840 began as a few strokes of a graver no wider than a hair.

 

At smaller sizes the same face must open its counters and thicken its hairlines; the engraver adjusted every size by hand. Variable optical sizes now make that adjustment automatically, which is why Night pins the display face to a sturdier size: bright strokes on black need the extra weight that small type once received.

“Typography exists to honour content.”

After R. Bringhurst
